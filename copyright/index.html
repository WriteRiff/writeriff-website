Copyright Policy

Last updated November 21, 2026

The short version. Only add, narrate or share writing you wrote yourself or have permission to use. If you believe something in WriteRiff infringes your copyright, tell us and we'll act on it. If your work was removed by mistake, you can ask for it back.

This policy explains how Beckman Studios LLC, doing business as WriteRiff ("WriteRiff", "we", "us"), handles copyright. It's part of our Terms of Use. We respect the rights of writers, publishers and everyone who creates, and we expect everyone who uses WriteRiff to do the same.

You're responsible for what you add

WriteRiff is a place for your own writing. We don't check what people add before they add it, and we don't own it. You are responsible for everything you upload, import, paste, narrate, turn into an audiobook or share in WriteRiff, and you promise that you own it or have all the permission you need to use it that way.

That means you shouldn't add, narrate or share:

  • someone else's book, story, ebook, audiobook, script or article, unless it's in the public domain or the rights holder has given you permission;
  • translations, scans or copies of published works you don't hold the rights to;
  • pictures, artwork, characters, logos or music that belong to someone else, unless you have permission.

Before your first AI narration and before you first share anything with other people, WriteRiff asks you to confirm that you own the rights to the text or have permission. We keep a record of that confirmation (the date and the wording version).

How to report copyright infringement

If you believe something shared or published through WriteRiff infringes your copyright, you can:

  • use Report → Copyright on the shared or published content in the app (this starts a notice for you), or
  • send a written notice to our designated copyright agent, below.

To be a valid notice under the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA), your notice must include:

  • your physical or electronic signature (typing your full name works for email);
  • the copyrighted work you say is being infringed (or, if there are several, a representative list);
  • the material you say is infringing, and enough information for us to find it in WriteRiff (for example the Story title, the writer's display name, and where it appears);
  • your name, postal address, phone number and email address;
  • a statement that you believe in good faith that the use isn't authorized by the copyright owner, its agent or the law;
  • a statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act for them.

What happens after a notice

1. We review it. We check that the notice is complete and that it points to material in WriteRiff.
2. We act quickly. If the notice is valid, we remove or disable access to the material. In practice this means the shared or published copy is hidden from everyone else. Your own private copy of your writing stays with you, so you don't lose your work.
3. We tell the account holder. We let the person who shared the material know what was removed and why, and send them a copy of the notice so they can respond. Their notice may include your name and contact details.
4. We keep a record. We record the notice and our decision, including whether it counts toward our repeat-infringer policy below.

Reports made in the app go to the WriteRiff team for review. A report alone never deletes anyone's writing.

Counter-notices: if your work was removed by mistake

If your material was removed and you believe that was a mistake, or that you have the right to use it, you can send us a counter-notice at the address above. It must include:

  • your physical or electronic signature;
  • what was removed, and where it appeared before it was removed;
  • a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith the material was removed or disabled as a result of a mistake or misidentification;
  • your name, postal address and phone number;
  • a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the US Federal District Court for the judicial district where your address is (or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which WriteRiff may be found), and that you'll accept service of legal papers from the person who sent the original notice, or their agent.

When we receive a valid counter-notice, we send a copy to the person who sent the original notice. Unless they tell us within 10 business days that they have started a court action to stop the infringement, we restore the material within 10 to 14 business days after we received the counter-notice. A takedown reversed this way doesn't count toward the repeat-infringer policy.

False notices

Please make sure a notice or counter-notice is accurate. Under US law, anyone who knowingly misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages, including costs and lawyers' fees. We may stop accepting reports from people who often send notices that are clearly unfounded.

Repeat infringers

We keep a count of upheld copyright takedowns for each account. A takedown is "upheld" when we removed material after a valid notice and it wasn't restored after a counter-notice.

  • After an upheld takedown, we warn the account holder.
  • After three upheld takedowns, we suspend the account's sharing and publishing, and we may close the account.
  • If we close an account, we'll let the account holder download their own writing first, unless the law or a court order prevents it.

Serious cases

Three strikes is our normal rule, not a promise. In serious cases, we may suspend an account's sharing and publishing at once while we investigate, and we may close the account immediately, at our discretion, even on a first notice. Serious cases include:

  • uploading or sharing a whole copyrighted book, story or audiobook, or large parts of one;
  • pirating or sharing other people's work for money;
  • uploading or sharing again material we already removed;
  • sending a counter-notice that's false or made in bad faith;
  • several notices about different works arriving at once.

While we investigate, your writing stays yours and stays on your devices; only sharing and publishing are paused. Where we reasonably can, we'll tell you what we found and give you a chance to reply before we decide. If we find it wasn't serious, we lift the suspension.

Reports from the European Union

During the beta, WriteRiff is offered only in the United States. If EU law applies anyway, including the Digital Services Act, anyone can tell us about content in WriteRiff they believe is illegal, including copyright infringement. Use Report in the app or email support@writeriff.com (our single point of contact; we read messages in English). Your notice should include:

  • why you believe the content is illegal;
  • exactly where it is in WriteRiff;
  • your name and email address (not needed for reports about child sexual abuse);
  • a statement that you believe in good faith that the information in your notice is accurate and complete.

We'll confirm we received it, review it carefully and objectively, and tell you what we decided and how you can challenge the decision. If we restrict someone's content or account, we tell them what we did, why, the facts and the rule or law we relied on, whether any automated tool was involved, and how they can challenge it. They can ask us to look again within six months by replying to our message or writing to support@writeriff.com, and they may also use a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body or go to court.

Other kinds of reports

  • Trademarks, privacy, harassment or other illegal content: use Report in the app or email support@writeriff.com.
  • Child sexual abuse material: report it in the app right away. We remove it, preserve it as the law requires and report it to the US National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC).

Changes to this policy

When we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top. If a change is significant, we'll tell you in the app or by email before it takes effect.
